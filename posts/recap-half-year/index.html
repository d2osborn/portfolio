Life Since Graduation

11-08-2026

Hi y'all! Welcome to the third entry of my blog. I know it's been awhile since I've posted anything haha, but I just wanted to share a little about what has happened and what I've been working on - or at least tried to - since my last post!

Where We Last Left Off

So the last blog post I made was discussing Chapter 1 of Bayesian Data Analysis (BDA) by Gelman et al., back in July. Obviously this seems like awhile now - about 4 months at the time of this writing - so it's perfectly reasonable for one to ask why I stopped covering the rest of the chapters. The honest answer is that it was a bit of burnout and trying to work on multiple things at once. Which is exactly what I want to cover with this post - basically what it was that I was and have been working on!

Graduate School?

So, to start, I've been working on graduate school applications for the upcoming Fall 2027 cycle. So since July, I've spent a good amount of time researching programs, refining my resume/CV, and working on my application essays. As you can probably gather from my portfolio and resume, I have a particular fondness for Bayesian statistics, so graduate school seemed like a natural next step to explore that interest more formally and in greater depth. With that in mind, I focused exclusively on Statistics programs that either had a strong Bayesian focus or at least offered multiple courses in the subject. This has been one of the bigger projects I've been working on since July, but I'm happy to say that the need to pursue graduate school isn't quite as pressing as it once was - which I'll get into in a bit.

Catch Probability Project

So besides applying to graduate school, I had also been trying to work on my own personal baseball data science problem. More specifically, I was trying to "fix" the catch probability issue of attaching blame only to one fielder, and especially when they weren't the fielder who actually made the attempt - e.g., a CF calling off the LF and misses it, but the LF ends up getting the loss in OAA. So using publicly available Statcast data, I was trying to work on a new approach of modeling catch probability. At least, that was the goal. For the same reason as the other projects I worked on, I ended up going on what is essentially a hiatus for this project. I did make some progress with feature engineering, but that was as far as I got. I hope to come back to it in the future when I have the time to just focus on it without other pressing projects to work on. For reference, here is the GitHub repository if you care to see the little progress I had made haha!

Saberseminar

I also had the pleasure of attending the Saberseminar conference in Chicago, in late August, and I truly believe that this is one of the most memorable and influential experiences I've had with respect to my career path. At the conference, I had the privilege of meeting with a number of people already in the baseball analytics industry, and I got to learn a bit about them, their career paths, and about how their R&D group operates and the kind of work they do. Getting to pick the brains of these people and also share my own goals and passions is very valuable, and for anyone who is interested in working baseball, I highly, highly recommend attending Saberseminar.

Big Data Bowl

This is more recent, but I am also participating in the 2027 NFL Big Data Bowl . I think the prompt this year is really interesting - essentially using combine tracking data and translating that to outcomes at the NFL level - so I knew that this kind of project is a perfect opportunity to further explore some statistical techniques and methodologies I'm interested in. It also just provides the kind of data that isn't publicly available. I'm also a huge football fan - GO HAWKS! Submissions are due sometime in January, so I'll be sure to share what I come up with then - probably on my Twitter / Bluesky or LinkedIn , or all three.

A New Job?

I am also excited to share that I will be joining the Texas Rangers in their R&D department as a Baseball Analytics Apprentice this coming January!!! I cannot put into words how excited I am to have received this opportunity, and as you can see from my portfolio and blog posts, I am extremely passionate about baseball analytics, so this is an unbelievably huge opportunity for me. Really everything I have worked for was for an opportunity like this, and I only hope to continue learning more and more as I move forward in my career. I'm really grateful to the Texas Rangers for giving me this opportunity, and also just everyone else who has supported me and helped me get here throughout - especially my wonderful girlfriend, my family, friends, the UCSD baseball team, professors, baseball people, and the baseball community on Twitter!!!

That's all I have to share at the moment, but thank you so much for reading!
